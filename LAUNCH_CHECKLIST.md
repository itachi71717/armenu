### Pilot prep
- [ ] List of 30 target restaurants in your city · names, addresses, manager names if known

- [ ] Scanning kit assembled: lazy susan, white paper, softbox light, credit card for scale
- [ ] One sample dish scanned + uploaded so demo is fully working before you walk in
- [ ] Leave-behind A5 one-pagers printed (50 copies)

### First three days

- [ ] Day 2: 10 cold walk-ins. Same.
- [ ] Day 3: 10 cold walk-ins. Same.
- [ ] **Goal: 30 demos given, 5 follow-ups booked.**

## Anti-checklist (do NOT do these)

- ❌ Don't build the comparison-mode feature until 20+ paying clients ask for it
- ❌ Don't add billing automation until manual invoicing breaks
- ❌ Don't hire before clients are paying for the hire
- ❌ Don't redesign the website in month 3 because you're bored — focus on sales
- ❌ Don't do free trials at scale — only the first 5 ever, never again
- ❌ Don't quote prices over the phone or via WhatsApp text — always in person, after a demo
- ❌ Don't promise features you haven't shipped — under-promise, over-deliver

---

## When to hire your first developer

**Trigger:** You have 20+ paying clients AND you are personally spending more than 8 hours/week on platform issues OR 3+ clients have asked for a feature you can't deliver.

**Not before.** Your job until then is sales, scanning, and customer love. Code is already built — let it earn before adding to it.

---

**Remember:** the platform is the easy part. It's already built and works. The hard part is convincing 50 restaurant owners to say yes. Every hour spent improving the website past "good enough" is an hour not spent in front of a customer.

**Go sell.**
